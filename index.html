01 / ABOUT HER

A mind beyond
her time.

Ada Lovelace (1815–1852) was an English mathematician and writer who is widely recognised as a pioneer in the history of computer programming. Born Augusta Ada Byron in London, she was the daughter of poet Lord Byron and Anne Isabella Milbanke. Her mother encouraged her to study mathematics and logic. During her intellectual journey, Ada met Charles Babbage, an inventor who designed a proposed mechanical computing machine called the Analytical Engine.

In 1843, Ada translated an article about the Analytical Engine and added detailed notes of her own. These notes described a method for calculating Bernoulli numbers, often considered an early computer program. Her vision went beyond calculations: she believed machines could manipulate symbols and potentially create music when given suitable instructions. Although the Analytical Engine was never completed during her lifetime, her ideas anticipated important concepts in modern computing. Today, Ada Lovelace inspires students, mathematicians and technology enthusiasts around the world.
